## SPRING BREAKUP — SCENE 1: Breakfast, The Gas & The Peter Transformation

### `INT. LING-EIDOLESSA HOUSE — KITCHEN — MORNING`

Sunshine pours across quartz countertops and a six-burner commercial range. Sizzling bacon fat pops against stainless steel. 

At the breakfast bar, HALEY slumps over a bowl of dry organic granola. Her hips are slim beneath vintage cargo shorts; her ponytail droops. A battered duffel bag gapes open on the kitchen stool, spilling faded band tees.

Across the island sits STELLA. Copper-red fox fur gleams beneath the pendant lights. She wears oversized tortoise-shell reading glasses, an oversized vintage MIT hoodie, and tiny running shorts that reveal her sleek, athletic legs. A stack of graduate-level number theory texts and three ten-sided dice flank her plate of untouched blueberry waffles.

STELLA
(scribbling in a leather-bound notebook)
If Toshi runs the second-order Markov chain on our saving throws, Barry's dwarf cleric survives the dungeon cellar with twelve hit points. Assuming zero dragon breath.

HALEY
(groaning)
Stella, you're an eighteen-year-old fox girl who has half the varsity girls' volleyball team sliding into your DMs at red lights. Why are you spending spring break doing matrix algebra in a basement with Snot?

STELLA
(pushing glasses up her snout)
Volleyball players don't understand second-order Markov chains, Haley. Number theory isn't a hobby. It's an aesthetic. Plus Snot brought Cool Ranch Doritos from the regional distribution plant. Uncut flavor dust.

At the range, FRANCINE flips a pan of eggs with one hand and packs a vintage leather medical satchel with the other. She wears high-waisted linen trousers, a crisp white blouse, and a stethoscope slung casually around her neck.

HALEY
Mom, tell her to go to Daytona. Jeff's van has room for two coolers and three surfboards. If she comes, the cops won't search the spare tire.

FRANCINE
Haley, your sister has her passions. And I have mine. Right now, mine is family triage.

ROGER slides into the kitchen through the pantry door on roller skates, wearing a lime-green Hawaiian shirt, oversized sunglasses, and a wide-brim straw sun hat. He holds an umbrella-topped pineapple hurricane.

ROGER
Did somebody say Daytona? Because Scotch Bingeington has eighty kegs of Natty Light arriving at the border via diplomatic pouch. We're throwing a foam party in the rec room, girls. Cover charge is twenty bucks or a signed medical release!

FRANCINE
(pointing her spatula at Roger)
No foam parties in the rec room, Roger. And stay away from the neighbor's pool. Stan lives across the street with his whole starch-ironed family and I don't need neighborhood watch calling Langley code enforcement again.

ROGER
Oh please. Stan's at Denny's eating lukewarm scrambled eggs and pretending he understands football. He doesn't have the stones to cross the cul-de-sac.

Francine snaps the satchel shut with a loud brass click.

HALEY
Wait, where are you even going with a stethoscope?

FRANCINE
Nana and Papa's. Bah-Bah had his hernia surgery on Tuesday. The incision is fine, but the post-op gas buildup is catastrophic. His belly looks like a submerged basketball. The surgeon said the nurses at the clinic refused to do the physical decompression anymore.

HALEY
Decompression?

FRANCINE
Farting him, Haley. I have to go over there three times a day and fart my father. You press down between the second and third rib, rock your palms counter-clockwise, and squeeze the air out like an accordion. If I don't, the recliner warranty is voided.

From the hallway archway comes a sound like an anchor dropping into deep harbor silt.

A low, resonant GURGLE vibrates the kitchen island. The salt shaker creeps half an inch to the left.

RAINEE steps into the kitchen. Her long lavender hair spills over an emerald silk robe. Her silver Eidolessa eyes are slightly crossed. She presses both hands against her lower abdomen, moving with the wide, stiff gait of an offshore trawler.

RAINEE
(voice strained, breathless)
Franny... the kimchi pancakes from midnight. They're... they're staging a coup.

FRANCINE
(rushing over)
Babe, sit down. Take a breath.

RAINEE
I can't sit down. If I sit down, pressure builds. It's... oh god, it's bubbling.

Rainee clutches the edge of the breakfast island. Her hips tremble. Her silhouette begins to ripple—flesh and fabric shifting under sheer internal pressure.

A sudden, sharp CRACK echoes through the room—a dry, percussive BRAAAP that shakes the pendant lamps.

White smoke doesn't appear, but the physical laws of the room bend. 

Rainee's jaw squares out with a sudden, meaty thud, sprouting a cleft chin deep enough to hold a quarter. Her delicate nose rounds into a fleshy bulb. Round tortoiseshell spectacles materialize over wide, cartoon-round eyes. Her silk robe snaps and reweaves itself into a crisp white collared shirt and emerald-green trousers. 

Her backside swells outward with hydraulic finality, expanding into a massive cantilevered dome that immediately snaps the rungs of a barstool.

Stella's ten-sided die rolls off the counter and drops into the sugar bowl.

HALEY
(dropping her spoon)
What the hell just happened to her face?!

ROGER
(taking a long sip through his straw)
Ah. The Peter protocol. Happens every third equinox. Somebody get her a Budweiser and a cutaway gag.

RAINEE-PETER
(booming baritone, New England drawl)
Holy crap, Francine. This is worse than the time I tried to teach the choir how to riverdance at Nana's church.

FRANCINE
(eyes wide, admiring)
Rainee... honey?

RAINEE-PETER
(patting her massive gut)
Look at this, Fran. I'm bloated like a Macy's float. My center of gravity is completely in Massachusetts. 

FRANCINE
(smiling slowly, clicking her pen)
You're not just bloated, babe. You're a clinical emergency. And I am on call.

## SPRING BREAKUP — SCENE 2: The Entry Hall & The Flatbed Gag

### `INT. LING-EIDOLESSA HOUSE — ENTRY HALL — CONTINUOUS`

Nurse Francine Deluxe shoulders the front door open, overnight bag in one hand, Rainee-Peter's arm in the other. 

Francine has changed: a starched white vintage nurse uniform, red-cross pin on the lapel, white cap pinned into her blond hair, white sensible clinic sneakers with rubber soles.

Rainee-Peter has to turn sideways to clear the mahogany frame. The fallen family photo from the coat rack crunches under one size-twelve sneaker. Glass spiders outward across Stan's old college track medal. Neither woman looks down.

FRANCINE
(to the house, cheerful)
We're off! Girls, don't burn it down! Roger, no portal hookups in the attic!

RAINEE-PETER
(booming baritone, grand gesture)
Wait. Do I fit in the car, or do we need to call a flatbed?

FRANCINE
You fit. You just have to believe.

### `EXT. LING-EIDOLESSA DRIVEWAY — CONTINUOUS`

The sedan sits low and patient on the concrete, like a dog that knows it is about to be asked for something unreasonable. 

Across the street, STAN SMITH stands by his mailbox in creased denim jeans and white crew socks, holding a weed-whacker. He freezes, staring across the asphalt as the front door opens.

Francine ignores him entirely, opening the sedan's passenger door with a bullfighter's flourish.

Rainee-Peter regards the bucket seat. Then her hips. Then the seat again.

RAINEE-PETER
This is an act of aggression against American foam manufacturing.

She lowers herself in. 

The rear suspension sighs—a metallic hiss of compressing oil and metal. Somewhere underneath the carriage, metal says a word.

Across the cul-de-sac, Stan drops his weed-whacker. It buzzes once against his lawn and dies.

RAINEE-PETER (CONT'D)
Okay. Okay okay okay. We're in. This is fine.

FRANCINE
(sliding into driver's seat, buckling up)
You're not in. You're on. There's a difference, and it's my suspension.

## SPRING BREAKUP — SCENE 3: The Drive Over & The Taco Bell GPS

### `INT. FRANCINE'S SEDAN — CONTINUOUS`

Rainee-Peter's knees press against the glove compartment lid. The fabric of her green slacks stretches drum-tight across the console.

The passenger seatbelt stretches outward, groaning against the b-pillar spring. It locks. It strains. It gives up with a dry metallic rattle.

Francine looks at the belt. Looks at Rainee. Looks back at the belt.

FRANCINE
Suck it in.

RAINEE-PETER
I am sucking it in. This is sucked in. This is the sucked-in-est I get, Francine. My spleen is currently behind my collarbone.

A low, subterranean gurgle rolls through the floorboards. The seatbelt retracts itself with a polite whizz all the way into the pillar, as if it has decided to leave the car entirely.

On the dashboard, the passenger airbag light flickers once, thoughtfully, and stays lit.

FRANCINE
(shifting into drive)
You know what? We're not doing the belt. We're doing prayer.

She pulls out of the driveway, swinging wide to clear the curb. Rainee-Peter adjusts the rearview mirror with two thick fingers; the glass now aims squarely at her own cleft chin.

The dashboard GPS screen wakes up with a blue chime.

GPS VOICE
(digital chime)
In four hundred feet, turn right onto Cherry Street.

RAINEE-PETER
Turn right. Yeah. Sure. Hey, does this thing do custom voice packs? Can I be the voice?

FRANCINE
You are not the voice of my GPS.

RAINEE-PETER
(dropping chin, nasal and majestic)
In four hundred feet... you will turn right. And there will be a Taco Bell. And it will be a sign from the Lord.

FRANCINE
I'm turning the voice off.

RAINEE-PETER
You can't. I'm the voice now. This is the transformation. It's permanent until the flatulence abates.

Francine reaches across the gear shift for the volume knob. Rainee-Peter catches her wrist, gently, with a hand the size of a dinner plate.

They look at each other at the red light. 

Francine snorts first. Then Rainee-Peter laughs—a rattling baritone honk that vibrates the rearview mirror and sets off the parking sensor chime.

FRANCINE
(wiping a mascara tear)
I hate that I'm enjoying this.

RAINEE-PETER
As your attending spouse, I'm prescribing one fart and a large iced coffee.

FRANCINE
You're not the attending. I'm the nurse. That was the whole bit.

RAINEE-PETER
(settling back against the headrest, smug)
The bit has evolved, Franny. The bit has grown cheeks.

## SPRING BREAKUP — SCENE 4: The Arrival & The Recliner Rumble

### `EXT. NANA AND PAPA'S HOUSE — DAY`

A tidy, brick-and-siding suburban rancher with a brass wind chime and a garden hose coiled on the lawn like a sleeping snake. 

MA MA LING stands on the porch steps, arms folded across an embroidered cardigan, mid-sentence before Francine's sedan has even cut its engine.

Through the large double front window: BAH-BAH sits marooned in a brown vinyl recliner, both hands pressed flat against his protruding stomach. The television blares an afternoon game show at punishing volume.

MA MA LING
You brought another one.

FRANCINE
(stepping out, smoothing her nurse uniform)
I brought a patient, Ma. Two patients. I'm a licensed professional now.

MA MA LING
(eyebrows climbing)
You are a professional something.

The sedan passenger door opens with a metallic pop. 

Rainee-Peter unfolds from the interior in distinct geometric stages, like an oversized folding lawn chair. Her green trousers catch the midday sun; her massive rear silhouette eclipses the hydrangeas.

Ma Ma Ling watches the entire three-stage emergence without blinking.

MA MA LING (CONT'D)
(flat, unimpressed)
Rainee. You got fat.

RAINEE-PETER
(booming baritone, deeply wounded)
I got gas, Ma! There's a medical difference!

### `INT. NANA AND PAPA'S HOUSE — LIVING ROOM — CONTINUOUS`

Inside, Bah-Bah shifts two inches in the brown vinyl recliner. The recliner's wooden joints groan in protest.

Rainee-Peter pauses at the threshold, head turning toward the window, nostrils flaring as if catching a scent on the wind.

Bah-Bah looks up from the TV screen. Through the double-paned bay window, across fifteen feet of manicured lawn, two bloated patients lock eyes in profound, unspoken solidarity.

A low, synchronized GURGLE rolls out of the house and echoes off the porch—a stereo rumble like distant thunder operating on a strict municipal schedule.

MA MA LING
(already retreating toward the kitchen)
Not on the good couch. I mean it, Francine! Not on the good couch!

Francine steps through the doorway. Her nurse dress does not have sleeves. 

She rolls them up anyway.

FRANCINE
(to the living room, to the hall, to the sky)
Alright, boys. Drop your belts. Who's first?
