
# Episode Outline: Tapped Out (Main Story Only)

# Act 1: Setup and Distance
## Beat 1: Stella’s Independence
> Notes:
> Stella has been avoiding Francine, spending more time out of the house and exploring the world on her own; Francine feels left behind by her growing daughter. Klaus observes dryly that Stella’s “princess” era is giving way to independent womanhood, needling Francine’s anxieties as Stella’s priorities shift.
> Dialogue samples:
> Klaus: "Uh oh, your princess is growing up."

## Beat 2: Francine’s Deepening Fears
> Notes:
> Francine doom-scrolls a string of anxiety-inducing videos about kids leaving the nest and aging alone, which intensifies her fear that Stella is slipping away; this spiral leaves Francine depressed and primed to overreact. The emotional pressure becomes the powder keg that will misdirect and amplify Hayley’s later magic.

## Beat 3: The Schedule Prank
> Notes:
> Stella arrives to run the day’s schedule and shows up in a stylish, businesswoman “hot girl” look, signaling maturity and autonomy; Francine sees a resemblance to Aunt Gwen Tenfiner and recoils at the implied change. Stella’s prank reframes the “dutiful daughter” routine as performance, not obedience, which further rattles Francine.

## Beat 4: Stella’s Retaliation Prank
> Notes:
> At 9:30 pm, Stella follows through on a payback prank against Hayley by dusting itching powder over a $100 makeup kit; the sibling sting is petty but cathartic. The prank is the immediate spark that brings Hayley’s powers into play under heightened emotion.

# Act 2: The Transformation
## Beat 5: The Magic Accident
> Notes:
> Angry, Hayley calls on goddess power and frames a “nerdy teen boy fantasy” curse; at the same time, Francine privately wishes for her “little princess back,” which warps the spell’s intent. The magic misfires and channels primarily into Stella, “compressing” her into a nerdy female/teen-boy hybrid—“dorky boy in a can”—with a locked matrix and a nerd body sporting huge breasts.

## Beat 6: Trapped and Transformed
> Notes:
> Stella is stuck in the nerdy female/teen-boy form and cannot revert; montage vignettes show incongruous domesticity, including her cooking and a moment of self-love in the cube before release. The “matrix lock” explains the failure to snap back, and the scene contrasts preserved cognition with imposed behavior.

## Beat 7: Francine Takes Advantage
> Notes:
> Sensing opportunity, Francine leans in to keep Stella dependent, believing this form guarantees her “little girl” forever; she labels and defines the persona to cement it. Naming becomes manipulation through identity framing.
> Dialogue samples:
> Francine: "Ya know, you look like a nerdy dorky boy who got into his sister's underwear drawer and just couldn't stop. If I had a son I'd call them Steve, but with Rainee Fox genes it was always going to be daughters. Look and kinda smell like a Steve."

## Beat 8: Naming the New Persona
> Notes:
> Hayley twists the knife with a nickname—“BIG TIDDY FEMALE STEVE!”—turning the regression into a gag that unfortunately reinforces the imposed identity. The joke lands, but the label sticks, worsening Stella’s bind.
> Dialogue samples:
> Stella: "Don't you dare call me that!"
> Hayley: (thinking) "How progressive I am—I respect Big Tiddy Steve's pronouns."

## Beat 9: Stuck, For Now
> Notes:
> Stella attempts to revert but can’t; Hayley’s powers are drained and cannot undo the damage immediately. Francine doubles down on babying Stella, talking as if “beauty queen” restoration depends on Stella staying pliant in this in-between state.

# Act 3: Diagnosis and Stakes
## Beat 10: Rainee’s Explanation
> Notes:
> Rainee evaluates Stella and explains that higher brain functions are intact, but an identity overlay has been imposed; Stella’s body “believes” the nerd template and follows it. The path forward is psychological and magical: make the body believe she is Miss Stella again.
> Dialogue samples:
> Rainee: "It's kinda like when she was stuck as Lashonda Dyke Rainee-Roger—her higher brain functions are protected—but an identity has been imposed on her matrix, and her body has come to believe Hayley’s idea of a nerd, thus acts like one. We just need to get her body to believe she is Miss Stella, not this nerdy construct."

# Act 4: Confrontation and Collapse (Before the Strippers)
## Beat 11: Stella Realizes the Truth
> Notes:
> Stella notices patterns in Francine’s behavior—infantilizing routines, strategic triggers, and exploiting the nerd persona’s impulse weaknesses—and prepares to confront her mother. The evidence frames the conflict as love turning coercive through arrested development.

## Beat 12: Francine’s Confession
> Notes:
> Francine admits she’s terrified of losing Stella and that distance has already begun; her rationalization is control masquerading as protection. The confession reframes the fight as fear versus autonomy.
> Dialogue samples:
> Francine: "As long as you're in this form, you'll never leave momma's nest and you lack the will and stamina to oppose her."
> Stella: "I can't do this."

## Beat 13: Complete Collapse
> Notes:
> Francine pushes a final lever—emotional or magical—that knocks Stella’s willpower out; Stella collapses into the “Big Tiddy Steve Smith” teen-boy persona and begins acting like a classic mamma’s boy. The low point cements the need for outside intervention and inner reassertion.
